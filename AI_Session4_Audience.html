Module 01 · Artificial Intelligence

So… what is AI, exactly?

AI means teaching machines to think, learn, and make decisions — a little like humans do. But here's the honest version:

AI does not have a real brain. It doesn't "understand" like you do.
It finds patterns in data and uses them to make smart guesses.
  • It recognises your face 🔓
  • It translates languages 🌍
  • It recommends your next favourite song 🎵
⚙️ The core loop of every AI
📊

Data In

We give AI lots of examples and information.

↓
🔍

Finds Patterns

It looks for rules and connections inside the data.

↓
💡

Makes Guesses

It uses those patterns to give smart answers.

Learning from examples

How does AI learn?

AI doesn't go to school. It learns by seeing thousands of examples, over and over — just like you learned what a cat looks like.

Step 1
🐱🐱🐱

See many examples

Shown thousands of cat photos — not told any rules.

Step 2
🔍

Find the pattern

What do all cats share? Ears, whiskers, eyes. It builds its own rules.

Step 3
✅

Make a prediction

New photo? "This looks like a cat!" More examples = better guesses.

AI can learn from text, sounds, images, and numbers — each type of data teaches it something different.

Limits ⚠️

The challenges of AI

🌀

It can be confidently wrong

Trained on poor or biased data, AI gives wrong answers — sometimes very confidently. This is called a hallucination.

💔

No real understanding

AI can sound kind, but it doesn't feel kindness or sadness. It just follows patterns in language — no emotions.

💼

May change some jobs

Simple, repeated tasks may be automated. Some jobs will change because of AI.

🔒

Privacy concerns

AI often uses personal data — photos, voice, location, history. How that data is used & protected matters.

Always check AI's work. A confident answer is not the same as a correct answer.

Module 02 · Machine Learning

What is Machine Learning?

Machine Learning (ML) means teaching computers to learn from data and examples — so they get better at a task without someone programming every single step.

Instead of writing out all the rules, we show the computer many examples and let it figure out the pattern on its own.
🧪 Lab · Two ways to teach a machine about cats

Can you write the rules for "cat"?

Traditional programming means writing every rule by hand. Pick the rules you'd use, then test your rule-book on real cats.

🛠️ Your rule-book: IF … THEN "cat"
🤖

The Machine Learning way

Instead of writing rules, we show the machine thousands of cat photos. It spots the patterns on its own — and can recognise even unusual cats it has never seen.

A hairless cat has no fur, no obvious whiskers, and may be silent — it breaks hand-written rules. That's exactly why we need ML.
Side by side

Machine Learning vs normal programming

Feature📊 Machine Learning💻 Normal Programming
Learns on its own?✅ Yes❌ No
Rules made by?Data (e.g. cat photos)A human programmer
Handles new input?Better with more dataNot very well
Gets better over time?✅ Yes, with more data❌ Only if re-coded
Normal code follows exact rules — miss a step and it breaks. ML is handed data and finds the patterns itself. Both are useful — sometimes a simple rule is the best tool!
When rules aren't enough

Why do we even need ML?

🗻

Some problems are too big

Sorting millions of photos by content — we can't do that one by one. ML learns to do it automatically.

🕸️

Too many rules to write

Some situations are too complex to write every rule. ML handles cases we couldn't fully predict.

📈

It gets better over time

Like a student, ML improves as it sees more examples — products get smarter and more accurate.

But remember — ML needs lots of good data. For a tiny, simple, fixed problem, a normal rule can be faster and safer.
A real example 🩺

ML that helps doctors

🧑‍⚕️

Without ML

A doctor examines every X-ray or MRI by hand. With millions of images and too few specialists, it takes enormous time — and humans get tired.

🤖

With Machine Learning

A model trains on thousands of labelled X-rays — "Healthy" or "Problem detected." It learns to spot unusual shapes and dark spots, then flags new images: 🟢 Looks normal or 🔴 Review needed.

Important: the AI helps qualified doctors — it does not replace them. A human professional always makes the final call.
The relationship

AI vs ML — what's the difference?

AI is the whole brain — the goal of making computers act smart. ML is one powerful part of it — the part that learns from data.

All Machine Learning is AI — but not all AI uses ML. A traffic light rule "if red → stop" is AI-ish logic with no learning at all.

Teaching machines to see

Computer Vision is the branch of AI that helps computers understand images and videos — like our eyes and brain working together.

  • Cameras act like eyes 📷 — capturing images

  • Trained on thousands of images to recognise objects, faces, text & actions

Module 04 · Natural Language Processing

Language is everywhere

Natural language is how humans talk and write — full of feelings, slang and mistakes. It comes in two forms. Sort each example.

🎙️

Spoken language

Words we say — tone, pauses, accents. Comes in as sound waves. Can be messy: "umm", fast speech, mumbling.

✍️

Written language

Words we type — spelling, punctuation, emojis. Comes in as text characters. Also messy: typos, "u" for "you".

🎙️ Spoken
✍️ Written
Both carry the same ideas — but a computer handles them differently: one arrives as sound waves, the other as text characters.
🧪 Lab · Meaning from context

What does NLP do?

NLP helps computers read a sentence and figure out what it means — turn speech to text, translate, answer & summarise. The hard part? Words with many meanings. Use the context to pick the right one.

A person knows instantly whether "cool" means cold or awesome. A computer has to be taught the difference — that's why NLP needs lots of training.
It's already around you

Where NLP works for you

🤖

ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and voice helpers like Siri & Alexa — understand messy messages and reply like a human.

✍️

Writing helpers

Grammarly, Word & Gmail fix spelling, suggest words, and predict what you'll type next ("recieve" → "receive").

🌍

Translation

Google Translate reads the whole sentence for meaning — not just word-by-word — even from your camera.

📊

Summarise & answer

Turn a long article into a few lines, or answer a question from a page of text.

Good translation captures meaning, not a word-for-word swap. "Break a leg" shouldn't become a medical warning!

🧪 Lab · Break it into pieces

Tokenization

Before a computer can understand a sentence, it splits it into pieces called tokens (for now, think "words"). Type a sentence and watch it split.

Each card is one token the computer reads on its own.0 tokens
Without this step, the computer just sees one big blob of text. Tokens let NLP read word by word. (Advanced models split into word-pieces, but words are the friendly starting point.)
🧪 Lab · Predict the base form

Stemming vs Lemmatization

✂️

Stemming

Chops off word endings to reach a root — fast, but sometimes leaves an incomplete root that isn't a real word.

🧠

Lemmatization

Uses grammar rules to find the real base form — smarter and more accurate.
