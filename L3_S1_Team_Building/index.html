CONCEPT 1 · THEORY 🤝

A team is not just a group of people

A team is people who trust each other, share the work, and communicate clearly to reach a shared goal.

🎭

Clear roles for everyone

Nobody is unsure what they are supposed to be doing — and nobody does the same job twice.

💬

Open, respectful communication

Speak up, share ideas, and listen to understand — not just to reply.

👂

A habit of listening — really listening

The rarest and most valuable teamwork skill in the room.

⚠️ The classic failure: five people all doing the same job while nobody watches the clock. Roles are not decoration — they are how five brains stop tripping over each other.
CONCEPT 1 · THEORY 🧭

Great teams do not just happen

They are built on three principles that guide every interaction and every decision.

🎯

Clear roles & responsibilities

Everyone knows their unique contribution to the crew's success. If two people own it, nobody owns it.

🗣️

Open communication

Speak up, share ideas, and listen to understand — not just to wait for your turn to talk.

💚

Mutual support & respect

Trust each other, value different perspectives, celebrate wins together.

CONCEPT 1 · THEORY 👂

Active listening: the secret superpower

👀

Look

Make eye contact. Show you are present. Phone down, body turned towards the speaker.

🤐

Do not interrupt

Let the person finish before you speak. The best idea is often in the second half of the sentence.

🔁

Repeat back

“So what you are saying is…” — confirm you understood before you respond.

💚 Why it matters: a crew where people feel heard produces more ideas. A crew where people get cut off produces silence — and silence has no ideas in it.

MAKE IT SPECIFIC ✍️

“I will help my crew by…”

The whole value of the card is in this one sentence. Vague sentences help nobody. Compare:

❌

Weak

“I will help my crew by helping.”
“I will help my crew by doing my best.”
“I will help my crew by being good at teamwork.”

✅

Strong

“I will help my crew by keeping the timer and calling out when 5 minutes are left.”
“…by writing every idea on a sticky note so none get lost.”
“…by asking the quiet person what they think before we vote.”

🎯 The test: could a teammate watch you today and tick it off? If not, it is too vague. Rewrite it as an action.

CONCEPT 2 · THEORY 💡

What is creative thinking?

Creative thinking means coming up with new ideas — especially ideas that are different, surprising or unexpected.

🌪️

The best tool is brainstorming

A fast, free, judgement-free way to generate lots of ideas quickly.

📈

Quantity creates quality

The more ideas you make, the better your chances of finding a great one. You cannot pick a brilliant idea out of three.

🧠 The science bit: your first three ideas are the obvious ones everybody has. Idea 8, 9, 10 is where the interesting stuff lives — but you only get there if you keep going past “I am out of ideas.”
THE RULES 🧠

The 4 Brainstorm Rules

1️⃣

Go for many ideas

Quantity first — do not stop at two or three. Aim for 15+.

2️⃣

No judging yet

Every idea is welcome. Save the filtering for later.

3️⃣

Build on others

Hear an idea? Say “Yes, and…” to make it bigger.

4️⃣

Welcome wild ideas

Crazy ideas spark the best real ones. Go for it.

🚫 The killer phrases — ban these for the next 15 minutes: “that is silly” · “we tried that” · “too expensive” · “be realistic” · “it will never work”
ARCADE 🎮

Idea Rain 💡🚫

THE ENGINE 🔄

Generate → Select → Improve

This three-step loop is behind every great Changemaker solution. You will use it today — and again in every session after this one.

🌪️

1 · Generate

Many ideas, fast, no judging. Widen the field.

⭐

2 · Select

Dot vote, discuss the top few, and pick the most promising — not the perfect one.

🔧

3 · Improve

Take the winner and make it stronger. Then loop again if you need to.

⚠️ The most common mistake: doing Select while you are still in Generate. Judging and creating are two different brain modes — mixing them kills both.

ACTIVITY 2 · INDIVIDUAL · 10 MIN ⚡

Ten Fast Ideas

Goal: practise divergent thinking — generating many ideas quickly without judging or rejecting them.

Pick ONE everyday object: a pencil, a sock, a plastic bottle, a paper cup, a cardboard box.
On Miro sticky notes, list 10 different uses for it — one idea per note.
You have 10 minutes. Fast. No judging. Nothing gets erased.
Stuck? Ask: What else could it become? Could I use it a completely different way? What if I changed its shape?
✅ Why this works: it trains your brain to think beyond the obvious, builds the “go for many ideas” muscle, and gets you ready for the big team brainstorm.

SHARED RULES 📐

Web or Art — these three never change

👁️

1 · Contrast beats decoration

If a person at the back of the room cannot read it, it does not matter how beautiful it is. Dark on light, or light on dark. Never light on light.

🪜

2 · Hierarchy tells the eye where to go

One thing must be biggest. If everything is important, nothing is.

🎯

3 · One page, one action

What do you want a visitor to do? Join, sign, come to the meeting? Say it once, say it big.

🔗 Link back to teamwork: the Checker is the person who tests these three rules on your work before anyone outside the crew sees it.

THE HUB 🗺️

Four zones. Work through them in order.

Each zone has a job. Do not skip ahead — the whole point of the loop is that each zone feeds the next one.

🎭

Zone 1 · Roles

Every name + every role. Written down, visible, agreed.

❗

Zone 2 · Our Problem

ONE clear problem, in one sentence.

🌪️

Zone 3 · Brainstorm

15+ sticky notes. One idea per note. No judging.

⭐

Zone 4 · Chosen Idea

The winner after the dot vote — improved, not just picked.

45 MINUTES 👣

Step by step

1

Agree on Roles · 5 min

Each person shares their Role Card. Add your name + role to the Roles zone on Miro. If two people want the same role, the Leader decides — fast.

2

Pick Your Problem · 10 min

What real problem do students your age face at school or in your area? Write one clear problem in the Our Problem zone.

3

Brainstorm Solutions · 15 min

Use the 4 rules. Everyone adds sticky notes to the Brainstorm zone at the same time. Go for quantity — aim for 15+.

4

Select and Improve · 15 min

Dot vote on your favourites. Pick ONE, improve it, and add it to the Chosen Idea zone.

STEP 2 🔍

Choosing your problem

✅

Good problem signs

Affects students your age · happens at school or in your community · you have seen or felt it yourself · a solution could actually help people.

💬

Starter prompts

“Something that stresses students out is…”
“A problem in our school nobody talks about is…”
“Students in our area struggle with…”

PROJECTOR GAME 🎮

Is this a good crew problem? 🔍

Ten statements. Class votes with hands, then we tap the answer and read why. Play this before the crews choose theirs.

STEP 3 🌪️

Running your brainstorm

⚡

Everyone writes at the same time

No waiting for turns. Turn-taking is the slowest possible way to brainstorm — add your sticky notes fast.

📝

One idea per sticky note

Keep it short and clear so the crew can read it in a glance. Long notes do not get read.

➕

Build on what you see

Read others' ideas and add a “Yes, and…” version right next to it.

⏱️ Target: 15+ notes in 15 minutes. That is one note per person every 5 minutes — very achievable, and only if nobody is judging.
STEP 4 ⭐

Dot vote → Discuss top → Choose & improve

🔴

1 · Dot vote

Everyone gets 3 dots. Place them on the ideas you believe in. Silent, simultaneous, no lobbying.

💬

2 · Discuss the top

Take the top 3 and talk. Anyone can argue for a low-scoring idea — that is allowed.

🔧

3 · Choose & improve

Pick one. Then ask: what is the weakest part, and what one change fixes it?

💚 Remember: you are not looking for a perfect idea — you are looking for the most promising one. You will keep improving it across all five sessions.
PROJECTOR GAME 🎮

Dot Vote 🔴

Practise the vote before you do it for real. You have three dots. Spend them, then see how the rest of the crew voted.
