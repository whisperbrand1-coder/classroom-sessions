CONCEPT 1 🧱

Every effective presentation has the same three parts

From a TED Talk to a school pitch. Master this and you always know what to say and when.

1

🎣 Opening — the hook

Grab attention immediately: a surprising question, a bold statistic, or a short personal story that connects directly to your school improvement idea. 30 seconds.

2

💡 Main message

State the problem clearly and explain how your Session 1 concept solves it. Specific, confident, focused. This is the heart. 60 seconds.

3

🏁 Closing

A memorable one-sentence summary followed by a call to action — tell the audience exactly what you want them to do next. 30 seconds.

CONCEPT 1 · DELIVERY 🎙️

Your body communicates before you say a word

🎙️

Voice

Speak clearly — every word heard · moderate pace · project to the back of the room · vary your tone, never monotone.

🧍

Body language

Stand tall, feet shoulder-width · face the audience, not your notes or the screen · natural gestures on key points · eye contact with different people.

💚 Walk to the front confidently and your audience already believes in you. The first three seconds are decided before you open your mouth.

CONCEPT 2 🔄

Rehearsing and team presentation flow

🔄

Smooth speaker handoffs

Know exactly who speaks when. Transitions should feel natural and planned. A simple phrase — “I'll hand it over to Maya now…” — keeps the audience with you.

🏃

Rehearsal check

Practising out loud — not just in your head — is the difference between a good pitch and a great one. It catches awkward phrases and builds muscle memory.

⚠️ Reading in your head is about twice as fast as speaking. A pitch that takes 2 minutes to read silently will take nearly 4 out loud. This is why silent rehearsal always overruns on the day.

ACTIVITY 2 · GROUP · 20 MIN ⏱️

Timed Pitch Outline & Role Allocation

Materials: Activity_2_Pitch_Outline_Worksheet.docx · index cards · timer.

1 · Divide the pitch into 3 timed parts. Opening 30s · Main Message 60s · Closing 30s. Write the target at the top of each section.
2 · Assign each section to team members. Names on the index cards. Every member gets a speaking role.
3 · Write key bullet points on the cards. Short bullets, no paragraphs. Cards are memory aids, not scripts — if you read word-for-word the audience will notice.
4 · Do one timed dry-run. Note what is too long or too short and adjust.

HANDS-ON TASK · ALL TEAMS 🌐

Build your School Improvement page — with AI, professionally

One page. Built with an AI assistant, then checked and fixed by you. Nobody presents a page they cannot explain.

Step by step

1 · Write the brief first, on paper, using the four lines from the game above: what to build · your real content · the section order · the quality bar. Do not open the AI until the brief is written.
2 · Ask for one HTML file that opens by double-clicking. If it hands you folders, npm commands or a zip, say: “Give me a single self-contained index.html with the CSS inside a style tag.”
3 · Replace every invented word. Any sentence that is not about your staircase, your canteen, your corridor gets rewritten. If the AI invented a statistic, delete it.
4 · Put your real hook in the <h1> — the one your team chose in Activity 1, word for word.
5 · Ask it to explain three lines you do not understand. Then have a different team member explain them back. That is the test.
6 · Add one real photo of the actual place in your school — with alt text describing what is in it.
7 · Save it as index.html in your team folder and open it on a second device before you present.

The four checks before you call it done

⌨️

Tab test

Press Tab from the top. Can you reach the button and press Enter? If not, it is not a real link.

📱

Phone test

Drag the window narrow. Does text spill off the side? Nothing should.

🗣️

Read-aloud test

Read the page out loud. Any sentence that does not sound like your team, rewrite.

🙋

Explain test

Point at any line. Can somebody in the team say what it does? If nobody can, delete it.

🚨 The one rule: the AI builds the scaffolding, you own every word. In the presentation the trainer will point at one sentence on your page and ask who wrote it and why. “The AI did” is not an answer — but “the AI drafted it and we changed it because…” is a very good one.

PRACTICE 🏃

Three delivery essentials, then sign off

🧍

Posture & eye contact

Stand upright, open body language. Look up from your cards at different parts of the room.

🔄

Smooth handoffs

Rehearse transitions until they feel coordinated. Introduce the next speaker by name.

⏱️

Timed practice

Use a timer every run-through. Aim for exactly 2 minutes. Too short means rushing; too long means being cut off.
