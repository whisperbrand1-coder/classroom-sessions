CONCEPT 1 👑

Successful projects don't happen by accident

They need diverse strengths and clear rules for working together. Four roles, four different jobs.

👑

Leader

Keeps the team focused and facilitates the flow of discussion. Not the boss — the conductor.

📝

Scribe

Documents all ideas, decisions and action items. If it is not written, by next week it never happened.

⏱️

Timekeeper

Monitors the clock and keeps the group on schedule. The only person watching the time.

🎤

Presenter

Communicates the team's output clearly to the audience.

⚠️ The rule that makes roles work: if two people own something, nobody owns it. Assign each role to exactly one person, out loud, before you start.

CONCEPT 1 👂

Active listening means being truly present

👀

Pay full attention

Distractions aside. Phone down. Focus on the speaker.

🤐

Withhold immediate criticism

Let the idea land before you react to it.

❓

Ask clarifying questions

“Can you say more about that?”

➕

Build on others' ideas

“Yes, and we could also…”

💚 Why it matters for today: in Activity 2 every person pitches for one minute. If the team is not listening, four of the five ideas are lost before the matrix is even drawn.

CONCEPT 1 🌪️

Quantity first, then quality

Brainstorming is a two-phase process. The more ideas you generate, the better your chances of finding a great one.

1️⃣

No judgment

During generation, every idea is welcome — even wild ones. Criticism shuts creativity down.

2️⃣

Encourage unique ideas

Push past the obvious answers. The best ideas come from unexpected angles.

3️⃣

Combine & build

Can we merge two ideas? Can we make this one bigger, or much simpler?

🚫 Ban these for the next fifteen minutes: “that is a waste of time” · “we already know the answer” · “be realistic” · “that will never get approved”. Every one of them is a verdict, and verdicts belong to the second phase.

ACTIVITY 1 · INDIVIDUAL 🗺️

School Day Friction Map

On your own, map out the rough spots in your school day. Materials: notebook or paper, pen — or a document named Activity_1_Friction_Map_Worksheet.docx.

1

List 3 friction points

Walk through your typical day — morning arrival, hallway transitions, class periods, lunch/recess, dismissal — and write three specific moments that feel disorganised, wasteful or frustrating.

2

Write one idea per moment

For each friction point, one practical improvement idea. Even a rough one counts.

3

Select your strongest idea

Choose the single best one based on how much it helps the school community — that is the one you bring to your team.

4

Write your “why”

One sentence: why is this change necessary for your school community?

PROJECTOR GAME 🎮

Friction Hunt 🔍

THE TEST ✅

Three questions before you write it down

📍

Is it specific?

Can you name the exact place and the exact time? “The corridors are bad” fails. “The main staircase between every lesson” passes.

🔁

Does it repeat?

A one-off is not a friction point. You are hunting patterns — things that happen every day or every week.

🛠️

Could anything be done?

If nobody in the building could change it, it is not your project. Start where you can act.

⚠️ Do not blame people. “Students are slow” is not a root cause, it is a complaint. Ask why until you reach something in the system: one narrow staircase, one till, no signage, no bin.
REFLECTION 🪞

Before you bring it to the team

✅ Expected output: an individual map of 3 school friction points paired with initial improvement ideas — plus your one strongest idea with a clear “why”.

Reflection questions

Was it easier to find problems or to propose solutions? What does that tell you about problem-solving?
How realistic is your top idea? What would it actually take to make it happen at your school?
CONCEPT 2 ⚖️

Not every idea can — or should — be built

Teams filter ideas using two criteria. Together they find the sweet spot.

⚙️

Feasibility

How easy or realistic is it to execute with the resources, time and permissions you actually have?

🌟

Impact

How many students or teachers will genuinely benefit from this change?

🎯

The Sweet Spot

High Impact + High Feasibility = the ideal quadrant for your project selection.

HOW TO USE IT 📊

Two questions, two axes

➡️

Feasibility — left to right

“How hard is this to do?” Further right = easier, cheaper, fewer permissions.

⬆️

Impact — bottom to top

“How many people benefit?” Higher up = more students and staff affected.

🎯 Ideas that land in the top-right quadrant are your strongest candidates. Start there. The top-left quadrant is your vision, not your plan.
PROJECTOR GAME 🎮

Plot the Impact × Feasibility matrix 📊

CONSENSUS 🤝

A decision the whole team can support

Consensus is not “everyone's favourite”. It is a decision every person can get behind, even the ones who preferred something else.

⚠️ If the vote ties: do not vote again. Ask “which one could we start on Monday morning?” Feasibility breaks ties faster than argument.
ACTIVITY 2 · GROUP 🧩

Team Formation & Concept Selection Matrix

Materials: flipchart or large paper, markers, selection matrix template — or a document named Activity_2_Concept_Matrix_Worksheet.docx.

1

Form your team & assign roles

Groups of 4–5. Assign Leader (facilitates flow), Scribe (documents notes), Timekeeper (monitors schedule), Presenter (leads verbal sharing). Say them out loud.

2

Pitch individual concepts

1 minute each for your top idea from Activity 1, while the rest of the team practises active listening. No interrupting.

3

Plot ideas on the selection matrix

Draw a large 2×2 grid: Feasibility horizontal, Impact vertical. Place each idea with a sticky note, by team discussion.

4

Select your core team concept

Identify the ideas in the High Impact / High Feasibility quadrant. Take a team vote to choose the winner.

5

Draft unified statements

Write a clear, shared statement defining the exact school problem chosen and your team's proposed creative solution.

PITCH TIMER ⏱️

One minute each. Clear and focused.

Project this while each teammate pitches. The rest of the team listens — no interrupting, no verdicts, questions afterwards.

STEP 5 ✍️

Problem-and-Solution statement

One shared paragraph. Every member has to be able to say it. Use this shape:

“At [exact place], during [exact time], [which students] experience [what goes wrong] because [root cause]. We will [the mechanism], so that [measurable benefit].”
❌

Weak

“The corridors are bad so we will improve them.”

✅

Strong

“On the main staircase, at every lesson change, all year groups get stuck because up and down traffic share one narrow flight. We will mark up-lanes and down-lanes with floor tape and two signs per landing, so lesson changes drop from six minutes to three.”

REFLECTION 🪞

How did the team actually work?

✅ Expected output: assigned team roles + a scored Impact/Feasibility matrix leading to 1 selected team concept with a clear Problem-and-Solution statement.

Reflection questions

How did your team handle differing opinions on which idea was best? Was it easy to reach consensus?
Did your assigned role help keep the group organised and moving forward — or did you forget it after five minutes?
MAIN TEAM CHALLENGE · 45 MIN 🏁
🏗️

Design the School Improvement Concept

Your team now takes your selected idea and builds it into a full, detailed concept — ready to present. Document name: Main_Challenge_Concept_Blueprint.docx

PART 1 🎯

Define your problem and your vision

Before designing a solution, articulate clearly what is wrong and where you want to go.

📛

Initiative name

An official, memorable name. Short enough to fit on a poster header.

📍

Target time & location

The exact part of the school day, and exactly where it happens.

❗

Current issue

What is going wrong right now, in detail — and why it matters.

🔭

Vision statement

Paint the picture of the school day after your concept is implemented.

PART 2 🛠️

Structure your team execution

A strong concept includes a realistic plan for how your team will actually make it happen.

👥

Team roster & roles

Every member alongside their leadership role and responsibilities.

🪜

Step-by-step breakdown

How does your solution work? What happens first, second, third?

📦

Resources needed

Materials, permissions, space, and support from teachers or admin.

🌟

Projected benefits

The specific positive changes students and staff will experience.

⚠️ The question that kills weak concepts: “Who has to say yes for this to happen?” If you cannot name the person, your resources section is not finished.
