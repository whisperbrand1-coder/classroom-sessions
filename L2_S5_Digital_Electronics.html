PART 1 · CIRCUITS 🔌

A circuit is a closed loop

A complete path that lets electrons travel from the power source, through the components, and back again.

THE ONE BIG RULE

Complete the loop, or nothing happens 🔁

Think of it like a toy car track. If there is a gap, the car stops. If the loop is complete, the car keeps moving — just like electricity.

✅

Closed circuit

The loop is complete — electricity flows and the bulb lights up.

❌

Open circuit

There is a break somewhere — electricity stops and nothing works.

📱 Every device you own — phone, TV, laptop — has circuits inside that must stay closed to work.

TWO KINDS OF CURRENT

Direct and Alternating ➡️↔️

➡️

Direct Current (DC)

Flows in one direction only. Batteries produce DC.
Used in: phones, flashlights, remotes, laptops.

↔️

Alternating Current (AC)

Switches direction back and forth many times per second. Wall sockets deliver AC.
Used for: homes, appliances, factories.

🔌 Why your charger gets warm: it is converting AC from the wall into DC your battery can safely use. That conversion is not free — some of the energy escapes as heat.
THREE MORE PARTS

The ones that make circuits interesting ⚡

⚡

Resistor

Slows the current down — like a narrow section of pipe. Protects the LED from burning out. Measured in ohms (Ω); the coloured stripes encode its value.

💡

LED

Glows when current flows — but only in one direction. Long leg = positive (+), short leg = negative (−). Always pair it with a resistor.

🔋

Capacitor

A tiny, fast battery. Fills slowly and empties in a snap — like a cup tipped over. Used in camera flashes and power stabilisers.

Battery supplies energy → Resistor controls current → Capacitor stores a backup burst → LED lights up
🧪 You met two of these in Lab 1 — remove the resistor and the LED burns; flip the LED and nothing happens at all.

THE DECISION MAKERS

Logic gates 🔣

Logic gates are tiny digital circuits that make decisions using 1s and 0s. Each one takes inputs, applies a simple rule, and produces a single output — millions of times per second. They are the building blocks of every processor ever made.

💻 You already know these. In programming you have used AND, OR and NOT as operators. In electronics they work exactly the same way — just with electrical signals instead of variables.

Combine them and you get more

🚫

NAND = NOT + AND

Does AND, then flips it. Output is 0 only when both inputs are 1.

🚫

NOR = NOT + OR

Does OR, then flips it. Output is 1 only when both inputs are 0.

🤯 Functional completeness: you could theoretically build any digital circuit — an entire CPU — using nothing but NAND gates. That is why engineers love them.

HOW COMPUTERS THINK

The instruction cycle 🔁

Every task a CPU performs — even adding two numbers — follows the same three steps. Think of a chef following a recipe: read the line, understand it, then cook it.

📥

1 · Fetch

Collect the next instruction from memory.
The chef reads the next line.

🔍

2 · Decode

Work out what it means.
The chef understands what it means.

⚙️

3 · Execute

Carry it out and store the result.
The chef cracks the eggs and adds them.

ISA

The bridge between software and hardware 🌉

For software to talk to a CPU they need a shared language — that is the Instruction Set Architecture. It defines exactly which instructions the CPU understands: ADD, SUBTRACT, MOVE, COMPARE.

🧩

Why it matters

Without an ISA, software could not talk to hardware at all. Different CPUs (phone vs laptop) have different ISAs — which is why some apps will not run on every device.

📖

The analogy

The ISA is a shared recipe book. The chef (CPU) and the author (developer) must agree on what “ADD 2 eggs” means, or the dish comes out wrong.

SENSE · THINK · ACT

Not everything that moves is a robot 🤖

A robot is a machine that can sense, think and act — usually on its own. It is not just something that moves; it responds to what is happening around it.

👁️

Sense

Cameras, touch sensors or proximity detectors perceive the surroundings.

🧠

Think

A microcontroller runs code to process the data and make a decision.

⚙️

Act

Motors, wheels or arms respond — it moves, stops or changes direction.

🤔 Back to the icebreaker: is a dishwasher a robot? It senses (water level), thinks (a program) and acts (pumps and sprays). Argue it out — the answer is less obvious than it looks.

THE FIVE THINGS

What to remember 🎯

Circuits are loops. Closed = electricity flows; open = it stops. Every device you own depends on complete circuits.
Logic gates make decisions. AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR — tiny circuits that are the decision-makers inside every processor.
Computers speak binary. Text, images and sound are all 1s and 0s, and the CPU uses Fetch → Decode → Execute to act on them.
Embedded systems are everywhere. From your microwave to your car, a microcontroller that senses, decides and acts is inside nearly every device.
Robots sense, think and act. Cameras, accelerometers and proximity sensors feed a microcontroller that drives the motors.
