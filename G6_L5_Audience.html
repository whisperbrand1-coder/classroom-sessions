RECAP

What is a sensor — and why does a robot need one? 📡

A sensor is a robot's way of finding something out. Without sensors a robot is just a machine repeating the same movements with its eyes shut.

🙈

No sensors

"Drive forward 500 mm, turn right." It will do that whether the road is clear or there is a wall 100 mm away. It cannot tell.

👁️

With sensors

"Drive forward until something is ahead, then turn." Now the same program works in a room it has never seen.

💡 The big idea of the whole course: a sensor turns a robot from something that repeats into something that responds.
THE LOOP

Sense → Think → Act 🔁

Every robot ever built runs this loop, thousands of times a second, for as long as it is switched on.

📡

1 · Sense

Read the world. "Object 60 mm ahead."
At this point the robot has no idea what that means.

🧠

2 · Think

Compare the reading to your rule. "Closer than 100? → YES."
This is the only place a decision happens.

🦾

3 · Act

Move, turn, or switch the magnet. "Stop and turn right."
Then straight back to sensing.

🔑 Take away the middle step and you have a machine, not a robot. Sensing without thinking is just data; acting without sensing is just a wind-up toy.
RECAP

The Distance Sensor 📡

📤

It sends

The sensor fires an invisible signal straight ahead, like a tiny shout.

📥

It listens

If the signal bounces back, something is there. The time it took tells the robot how far.

That is why a distance sensor can give you a number in millimetres — and why it works before you touch anything.

🦇 Bats and dolphins do exactly this. It is called echolocation, and your robot borrows it.
BOOLEAN

A block that answers a yes/no question ✅❌

This block does not move the robot. It answers a question, and the answer is only ever one of two things.

FrontDistance found an object?

  object in front  →  TRUE   ✅
  nothing in front →  FALSE  ❌
🧠 A value that can only be TRUE or FALSE is called a Boolean. It is what every if and every wait until is really asking for.

THE ELECTROMAGNET 🧲

A magnet you can switch off

Sensing is only half a robot. Today it also has to pick things up and put them down.

HOW IT WORKS

Electricity in, magnetism out ⚡🧲

A wire is wound many times around a metal core — that coil is the electromagnet.
Send electricity through the wire and a magnetic field appears around it.
The field pulls on steel and iron — so a metal disk sticks to it.
Cut the electricity and the field disappears instantly. Whatever it was holding drops.
🔑 That last step is the whole point. A fridge magnet can pick a disk up but can never let it go. An electromagnet can do both — because you control the current.

YOUR TOOLS

Only two blocks — but the order is everything 🧱

🧲

Electromagnet ON

Creates the magnetic field. It attracts and holds a metal disk.
Use it when you want to pick a disk up.

⭕

Electromagnet OFF

Releases the field, so any held disk drops.
Use it when you want to place a disk down.

⚠️ The mistake everybody makes: switching ON before you have arrived at the disk, or OFF before you have arrived at the goal. The blocks are easy — the sequence is the challenge.
RECAP

What is an algorithm? 📜

A precise, ordered list of steps that always produces the same correct result. Not a rough idea — an exact recipe.

1️⃣

Sequence

Do this, then this, then this. Order matters.

🔀

Selection

if something is true, do one thing — otherwise do another.

🔁

Iteration

repeat steps instead of writing them out again and again.

🎯 Today's mission uses all three: a sequence to reach a disk, a selection to check the colour, and iteration because you do it three times.

THINK ABOUT IT

Three things that just happened 🧠

Magnet ON with no disk under you does nothing. Arriving comes first — always.
Dropping on the wrong colour does not score. The disk just sits there and you have to come back for it.
Fewer blocks is better. If you used ▶ ×3 instead of three separate forwards, you already found a loop.
⚡ That last one is optimization — the Lesson 4 idea. Same result, less code. Now go and build the real thing in VEXcode VR.
THE LONG WAY

The solution you were given 📏

Three functions, each one a list of exact measurements. This is just one of the three:

define getBlue
    drive forward 700 mm
    turn left 90 degrees
    drive forward 300 mm
    energize Magnet to boost
    drive reverse 200 mm
    turn right 90 degrees
    drive reverse 400 mm
    turn left 90 degrees
    drive forward 800 mm
    turn left 90 degrees
    drive forward 200 mm
    energize Magnet to drop
😰 Now multiply that by three — getBlue, getGreen, getRed — plus the main program. That is about 49 blocks, and every single number has to be right. Miss by 20 mm on the first turn and every step after it is wrong.
🤔 So here is the question: the robot has a Down Eye that reads the colour of the floor. Why are we counting millimetres to find a goal we could simply look at?

THE SHORT WAY

The same mission in a fraction of the blocks 👀

One function instead of three, because the colour is now something you pass in rather than something baked into the numbers.

define deliver (colour)
    energize Magnet to boost            // grab the disk
    drive forward
    wait until DownEye detects (colour)   // look, do not count
    stop driving
    energize Magnet to drop             // let go, right on the goal


when started
    drive forward
    wait until FrontEye detects an object
    stop driving
    deliver (FrontEye colour)              // then repeat for the next disk
📏

Fixed distances

~49 blocks
Breaks if anything drifts a few millimetres.
Three near-identical functions.

👀

Down Eye

~11 blocks
Drift does not matter — it stops on the colour.
One function for all three disks.

🎯 This is the whole course in one comparison. Lesson 4 said a fixed path breaks when the world changes. Here is the proof, inside the final project: ask the sensor instead of trusting a number.
