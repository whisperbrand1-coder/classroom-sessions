PART 1 · RECAP 🖥️

UI and UX — one more time

You will need both of these to talk about your app today.

TWO QUESTIONS

The difference in one line each 🎯

🖥️

UI — User Interface

How would the product look to the user?

Colours · fonts · buttons · spacing · icons. Everything you can see.

😊

UX — User Experience

How would the user feel while using it?

Easy or confusing · fast or slow · calm or annoying. Everything you feel.

🍽️ The restaurant test: UI is how the plate looks when it arrives. UX is whether you enjoyed the whole evening — the waiting, the ordering, the food, the bill.

PART 3 · LEARN 📊

Now let's learn PowerPoint

The tool you will use to show everyone what you built.

FIRST STEPS

Starting a new presentation 📂

Open Microsoft PowerPoint.
Click New to start a brand-new file.
Choose Blank Presentation — or pick a ready-made theme if you want colours chosen for you.
Save it straight away with your group's name, before you type anything.
💡 Which should you choose? A theme is faster and always looks tidy. Blank gives you full control. For a five-minute talk, a theme is usually the smarter choice.

FOUR RULES

How to make text people can actually read 👀

1️⃣

Big enough to read from the back

If you would squint at it from the last row, it is too small. Titles around 40, body text around 24.

2️⃣

One way to stand out, not four

Bold or a colour or a highlight. All of them at once just looks messy.

3️⃣

Six words per line, six lines per slide

A slide is a poster, not a paragraph. The details come out of your mouth, not the screen.

4️⃣

Dark text on light, or light on dark

Yellow on white is invisible from row three. Test it by standing back.

🚫 The number one mistake: reading your slides out loud word for word. If everything is written down, the audience does not need you.

PART 4 · SHARE 🎤

Now the scary bit

Standing up and talking. Here is how to make it easy.

FIVE RULES

How to present so people listen 🎤

Look at the people, not the screen. Glance at your slide, then turn back to the room.
Say more than is written. The slide is the headline — you are the story.
Speak slower than feels normal. When you are nervous you speed up without noticing.
Everyone in the group says something. Decide who says what before you stand up.
Practise once out loud. Once. Not in your head — out loud, with the timer running.
😊 Nervous is normal. Every single person who has ever presented anything felt it. It does not go away — you just learn to start anyway.
