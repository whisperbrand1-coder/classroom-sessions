CONCEPT 1 · THEORY 🎯

Great presenters aren't born — they're trained

Before you say a single word, answer three questions.

🎯

What do I want them to know, feel, or do?

Pick one of the three. A pitch that tries to do all three does none of them.

👥

Who is my audience?

What do they already know? A head teacher and a classmate need different words for the same idea.

💎

What is the ONE thing they must remember?

If they forget everything else, what survives? Write it down before you write anything else.

💚 Clear purpose = clear presentation. Always. Every confusing pitch you have ever sat through was confusing because the speaker had not answered these three questions.
THE SHAPE 🧱

Every great presentation has the same three parts

1

🎣 Opening Hook

Grab attention — a question, a surprising fact, or a story. You get about ten seconds before the room decides whether to listen.

2

💡 Main Message

Your solution, explained simply. The problem, then the fix. Not the other way round.

3

🏁 Closing

A call to action, or why it matters. The last sentence is the one they walk out with.

🎬 From TED Talks to product launches to a two-minute pitch to your head teacher — same shape every time. Learn it once and you never have to wonder what to say next.
CONCEPT 1 · DELIVERY 🧍

Four things your body is saying

🔊

Clear voice

Slow enough to be understood. Project — don't shout. Volume comes from breath, not throat.

🧍

Good posture

Stand straight, feet shoulder-width apart. Own the space you are standing in.

👀

Eye contact

Scan the room. Connect with 2–3 people for a few seconds each — not one person for three minutes.

🧘

Calm body

Limit fidgeting. Breathe. Pause on purpose — silence is powerful and almost nobody uses it.

THE RULES 🤖

Three rules for using AI in this project

✅

1 · AI drafts, you decide

Ask for five options, never one. Then say out loud why you rejected four of them. If you cannot, you have not read them.

🔍

2 · Check every number

An AI will happily invent “73% of students”. If you cannot point at where a number came from, delete it — a made-up statistic destroys a pitch on the first question.

🗣️

3 · Say it in your own mouth

Read the line aloud. If it does not sound like a person your age, rewrite it. The audience can hear the difference instantly.

🚨 The tell: long sentences, words like leverage, utilise, in today's fast-paced world, and three adjectives where one would do. That is not your voice — and everyone in the room can hear it.
CONCEPT 2 · THEORY ⏰

Time doesn't manage itself

Most students spend their time on what feels urgent — not on what actually matters.

🔥

Do Now

Urgent and important. Exam tomorrow. A task due today. Something blocking a teammate.

🗓️

Plan It

Important, not urgent yet. Prep for next week's pitch. This is the box that decides whether next week is calm or chaotic.

📵

Drop It

Neither. Endless scrolling. Re-reading a chat. Making a slide "a bit nicer" for the fourth time.

⚠️ The trap: Drop It jobs often look like work — tidying, re-formatting, arguing about fonts. Urgency is a feeling. A deadline is a fact. Only one of them should decide your afternoon.

HABITS 🧠

Three habits that beat the clock

🗂️

Break it down

Split any big task into 4–5 small, doable steps. Small steps = less stress, and you can see progress.

⏳

Set mini-deadlines

Give each step its own time. Don't leave everything for the last minute — leave nothing for it.

🎯

Practise within limits

Rehearse inside your actual time limit. Surprise timing on the day = a messy presentation.

The task: “Prepare a 3-minute pitch.” Break it into 4–5 ordered steps with rough minutes, on a Miro sticky note.

⚠️ The thing everybody gets wrong: the 3 minutes is the pitch, not the preparation. Preparing it takes far longer than three minutes. Your plan is for the prep.
💡 Each sticky note carries three things: step number · task · rough time. For example: Step 1 — Choose the main message — 2 min

HANDS-ON TASK · WEB GROUP 🌐

Build your crew's pitch page

One HTML file. No framework, no build step, no library. It opens by double-clicking it. This is your crew's prototype for the Main Challenge.

What you must ship

1 · Create index.html with a proper skeleton: <!doctype html>, <html lang="en">, <meta charset="utf-8">, <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1"> and a real <title>.
2 · One <h1> and only one — and it must be your actual hook from Activity 1, word for word.
3 · Three <section> blocks: The Problem · Our Solution · Meet the Crew. Each starts with an <h2>.
4 · A <ul> of exactly three benefits, and one <img> of your prototype with real alt text that describes it.
5 · One call to action — <a href="…" class="cta"> — styled as a button, at the bottom.
6 · A <footer> with the crew name and the five members.
7 · CSS in a <style> block: set a max width on the body (about 70ch), a background colour, a text colour, and give the CTA a :hover state.

How you will be checked

⌨️

Keyboard only

Press Tab from the top. Can you reach the CTA and fire it with Enter? If not, it is a <div> pretending to be a link.

🖼️

Break the image

Rename the image file. Does readable alt text appear? If it says “image” or nothing, fix it.

📱

Squash it

Drag the window down to phone width. Does anything overflow sideways? Nothing should.

🔍

Outline test

Add * { outline: 1px solid red } temporarily. Every box should look deliberate.

🤖 Where AI helps and where it does not: ask it to explain why a tag is wrong, or to generate the CSS for a hover state. Do not paste in a whole page you cannot read line by line — the Checker will ask you what any line does, and “the AI wrote it” is not an answer.

A slide nobody can read

Contrast is not a matter of taste. It is a ratio you can calculate, and it either passes or it fails. Today you run the real numbers on your own poster.

DIGITAL ART GAME 🎨

Contrast Lab — the real WCAG maths

HANDS-ON TASK · DIGITAL ART GROUP 🎨

Design your crew's pitch visual

One hero image plus a three-slide deck, in Canva or Figma. This is what goes on screen behind your crew during the pitch.

What you must ship

1 · Set the canvas properly — 1920 × 1080 for the slides, 1200 × 630 for the hero image. Guess the size and it will be blurry on the projector.
2 · A real type scale. Headline, subhead, body. The headline must be at least 1.6× the subhead, and the subhead bigger than the body. Write the three numbers down.
3 · Contrast that passes. Run every text/background pair through the Contrast Lab above. 4.5:1 for body, 3:1 for anything 24px or larger. Screenshot the number as proof.
4 · Slide 1 is your hook, and nothing else. Maximum 8 words. No bullet points, no logo soup, no clip art.
5 · One accent colour, used three times maximum. Pick it, name its hex, and stick to it across all three slides.
6 · Safe margins. Keep everything at least 5% from every edge — projectors crop, and the person at the back is looking through six heads.
7 · Export correctly — PNG for anything with text, JPG only for photographs. Name the files so the Organiser can find them.

The tests you must pass

⏱️

The 3-second test

Show slide 1 to someone for three seconds, then hide it. Can they say what it was about?

👀

The squint test

Squint until it blurs. The most important thing should still be the most obvious thing.

📏

The back-row test

Stand at the far wall. If you have to lean in, the type is too small — full stop.

⬛

The greyscale test

Turn it black and white. If it falls apart, you were relying on colour instead of contrast.

🤖 Using AI for images — the prompt has five parts: subject · style · composition · lighting · what to avoid. “A school corridor” gets you nothing. “A wide empty school corridor, flat vector illustration, strong left-to-right composition with space for a headline on the right, bright even lighting, no text and no faces” gets you something usable. Then re-prompt — the first image is never the one you ship.
⚠️ Leave room for the words. The most common mistake is generating a beautiful image that fills the whole frame — and then having nowhere to put the headline. Ask for the empty space in the prompt.
THE HANDOVER 🤝

The two groups have to actually meet

The page and the visuals are the same product. Fifteen minutes before the pitch, the two groups swap.

🎨

Art hands over

The exported files, correctly named, plus the hex codes and the three type sizes. Not a screenshot — the actual values.

🌐

Web hands over

The live page open on a laptop, plus the alt text they wrote for each image so Art can check it describes the right thing.

🔍

The Checker runs the test

Give someone from another crew one task — “find out who to contact”. Watch where they hesitate. Do not help them. That hesitation is your bug list.

💚 The reason this matters: the crew that hands over hex codes and font sizes finishes in five minutes. The crew that hands over “just match the colours” is still arguing when the timer goes.

FEEDBACK · 10 MIN 💬

One strength. One fix.

⭐

One strength

“I really liked how you…” — and then something specific they did, not a general compliment.

🔧

One fix
